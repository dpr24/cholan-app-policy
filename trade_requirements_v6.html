Introduction & Goals

The system automates options trading decisions for an index using a pullback-entry strategy anchored to momentum crossovers. Its goals are to produce deterministic entry/exit signals, size positions per configurable risk profiles, enforce operational safeguards (time windows, exposure limits, cooldowns), and maintain an auditable trade lifecycle.

Configuration & Defaults

Key numeric defaults used across the engine (examples):

  • Capital fallback: 1,000,000
  • Max trades per day: 2
  • Max trades per trend: 5
  • Consecutive loss limit: 3
  • Standard lot size for index options: 65
  • Zig-zag retracement levels: 70%, 60%, 55%

Environment-driven parameters (risk percentages, slippage limits, retry counts, time windows) are read at startup and provide runtime behavior controls.

Data Models & Persistence

Trades, trade-events, orders and order-events are persisted in a relational database. For each trade the system records entire entry parameters (entry price, stop loss, target) at the moment of entry to avoid ambiguity when auditing later.

Migrations must be applied before running the service; ensure database connectivity settings are correct when a migration fails.

Broker Integration and Order Payloads

The engine resolves the chosen instrument, builds the broker payload, and places or modifies orders through the broker's API. Important payload attributes include:

  • tradingsymbol (instrument label)
  • symbol token (numeric identifier when available)
  • quantity, order type (limit/market), product type
  • price, trigger price, stop loss, square-off and validity

Numbers are typically stringified to match broker API expectations. Prices should be rounded to the allowed tick size before submission to avoid rejections.

Order Lifecycle and State Management

The order lifecycle has preflight validation, placement (possibly retried), in-memory tracking, and event logging for audit. Key validation steps include:

  • symbol and quantity bounds
  • price > 0
  • risk-reward check when stop loss and target are provided: reward/risk must meet a minimum ratio
  • market slippage check against live last traded price

On placement success the engine records the order metadata in runtime state and emits an order-placed event. Market orders are used for fast exits; limit orders for controlled entries.

Position Management and PnL

Positions are normalized from broker data into a consistent shape that includes quantity, average price, current price and unrealized PnL. The percentage PnL is computed relative to the position notional:

PnL% = (unrealized PnL) / (|quantity| × average price) × 100

Total exposure is the sum across open positions of (quantity × average price). Exposure and positions are used by risk routines to decide whether an additional position may be opened.

Risk Sizing and Capital Management

Risk profiles combine a customer's selected capital, a per-trade percentage or explicit per-trade value, and a maximum capital utilization limit. Two common sizing methods appear in the engine (choose one canonical approach for production):

Per-share risk-based sizing

Interpretation: the per-trade risk percentage defines the maximum acceptable monetary loss.

riskAmount = capital × riskPercent / 100

pricePerShare = |entryPrice − stopLoss|

quantity = floor(riskAmount / pricePerShare)

Lot-based allocation sizing

Interpretation: per-trade risk translates to a purchasing budget; compute how many lots that budget can buy:

tradeCapital = min(maxCapital, perTradeAbsoluteValue or percentRiskCapital)

minCapitalFor1Lot = entryPrice × lotSize

rawLots = tradeCapital / minCapitalFor1Lot ; lots = floor(rawLots) ; quantity = lots × lotSize

Note: the first method sizes based on maximum acceptable loss, the second sizes based on capital allocation. They are not equivalent; choose one and document it clearly in the client UI.

Trade Limits, Cooldowns & Safety Gates

The engine enforces operational safeguards:

  • daily/weekly/monthly trade counters with enforced maximums
  • limits per trend to avoid overtrading in a single directional move
  • consecutive-loss tracking: after a configured number of consecutive losses, the engine activates a cooldown period measured in trading days

Cooldown duration is calculated by skipping weekend days and ending on market close time of the final trading day.

Entry Session & Gap Controls

Entries are allowed only during a configured intraday window (for example 09:45–14:45 IST). Additional controls include:

  • opening gap detection: if the opening price gap (percent) exceeds a threshold and market is within a stabilization window, new entries are blocked
  • news-driven risk multiplier: high-impact news can reduce position sizing (for example, a 50% reduction)

Strategy Orchestration (Entry/Exit)

The decision pipeline follows a sequential gate model. Typical gates:

  1. Market open and session time gate
  2. Trade limits and exposure gate
  3. Momentum crossover detection on a primary timeframe
  4. Multi-timeframe alignment confirmation on lower timeframes
  5. Contract selection and liquidity checks
  6. Cross-price anchoring and tolerance band check
  7. Final checklist aggregation — entry permitted only if all gates pass

After entry the system locks bias to prevent duplicate entries and increments counters; exit evaluation runs periodically to check partial/full-booking conditions and time-based force exits.

Indicator Calculations & Thresholds

MACD

Uses standard MACD with typical parameterization: fast period 12, slow period 26, signal period 9. A bullish crossover is identified when the histogram moves from negative to positive and the MACD line crosses above its signal line.

Pullback

Pullback percent is calculated as the retracement from recent swing low:

pullback% = ((close − low) / (high − low)) × 100

Zig-zag retracement thresholds typically used: first pullback ≥70%, second ≥60%, third ≥55%.

Candle Filters

Lower timeframe candle filters enforce patterns such as mixed-color presence across the last 6 candles (reject if all same color), doji/hammer heuristics, and rejection of gap entries where the candle opens directly deep inside the pullback zone.

Volume Strength

Volume strength compares current candle volume to an average of previous N candles (commonly 20): multiplier = currentVolume / averageVolume. Thresholds: ≥2.0 strong, ≥1.5 good, else weak.

Utility Formulas

  • Percentage change: ((current − previous) / previous) × 100 (returns 0 if previous is 0)
  • Trailing stop: max(entryPrice, currentPrice) × (1 − trailingPercent/100)
  • Monetary risk: |entryPrice − stopLoss| × quantity
  • Monetary reward: |exitPrice − entryPrice| × quantity
  • Risk/reward ratio: reward / risk (guard against division-by-zero)
  • Position sizing (risk-based): floor((capital × riskPercent / 100) / |entryPrice − stopLoss|)

Operational Keys & Side Effects

Runtime state and counters are stored in a fast key-value store. Typical keys and meanings (human-friendly):

  • Daily exposure amount
  • Daily/weekly/monthly trade counters
  • Open positions count
  • Consecutive loss counters and active cooldown record
  • Active risk profile override stored by the UI
  • Cross-price anchor entries for each bias

Daily counters typically expire after 24 hours. Cooldown records persist until their calculated end time.

Numeric Examples and Walkthroughs

Risk-based sizing example

Given capital 500,000 and per-trade risk 1.5%, riskAmount = 7,500. If entry is 180 and stop loss 150, per-share risk = 30. Quantity = floor(7,500 / 30) = 250 shares.

Lot-based allocation example

Given capitalSize 1,000,000 and per-trade percent 1.5% → tradeCapital 15,000. If entryPrice 150 and lotSize 65, min capital for 1 lot = 9,750. rawLots = 15,000 / 9,750 = 1.538 → buy 1 lot (65 qty).

Note: the monetary risk for that lot may be much lower than the tradeCapital; clarify whether tradeCapital is an allocation budget or maximum loss.

Errors, Retries and Observability

Order placement uses a limited retry policy with sleeps between attempts. All significant lifecycle events are logged in a structured log stream for audit. It is recommended to persist the event audit trail to durable storage to survive restarts.

Appendix: Edge Cases & Recommended Tests

  1. Verify database connectivity and run migrations with verbose mode when migrations fail.
  2. Add tick-size rounding helpers to guarantee broker-friendly price submission.
  3. Standardize on one position-sizing method and reflect its meaning in the UI.
  4. Persist runtime order state in a central store for multi-process deployments.
  5. Add startup-time validations for required environment keys to fail fast if broker credentials are missing.
  6. Create timezone unit tests for intraday gates to ensure correct behavior across deployments.
